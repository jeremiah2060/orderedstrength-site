What actually gets heavier

Everything else on this site is in service of one number going up.

We spend most of these pages proving that Jerry is honest. Honesty is not the product. It is what makes the product work, because a coach who flatters you cannot make you strong. This page is the other half: what happens to your body, and the one claim we refuse to make until we have earned it.

Three time scales

Strength is built in a set, a week and a block, and he works all three.

Inside one set

He watches the reps land, then writes the next one.

You finish a set and tell him how many you had left in the tank. That answer changes the next set while you are still standing over the bar. If the reps came apart, the load comes down. If they did not, the load goes up, or the target does. Most apps hand you numbers that were decided last Sunday and never look up from the page.

Why it matters for your body: the set that grows muscle is the one taken close enough to failure to be worth taking, and not so far past it that the next two sessions pay for it. That line moves every day. A fixed program cannot follow it.

Across one week

Enough work to grow, and a stop before it becomes a hole.

Muscle comes from doing more than your body is used to and then recovering from it. Both halves are real work and the second one is the half people skip. Jerry counts what each muscle has taken this week and cuts the session when the arithmetic says you are borrowing from next week rather than earning this one.

What this feels like: you will get weeks where he asks for less than you wanted to give. That is the same job as asking for more, done in the other direction, and it is the reason the load can keep climbing in month five.

Across one block

The reps come first. Then the plate goes on.

The weight does not move because a calendar said week four. It moves when you have shown the reps at the weight you are already lifting. Reach the top of the range, hold it there, and the plate goes on. That is double progression, and it is the least fashionable and most durable way strength has ever been built.

The number you watch: your estimated one rep max, per lift. It climbs because the sets you actually logged moved, not because a plan predicted that they would. That is the difference between a projection and a measurement, and this app only shows you the second one.

The claim we will not make

There is no before and after photograph on this site.

Results data none yet

We do not have a results dataset. The test group has not finished a full block, so any number we printed here about how much stronger anyone got would be invented. Inventing that number is the single most common thing in this category, and it is the exact behaviour the rest of this site exists to refuse.

When the data exists it goes on the record, next to the accuracy figures, and it includes the athletes it did not work for. A results page that only contains successes is an advertisement wearing a lab coat.

Until then the promise is narrower, and it is one we can keep on your very first session: every load he asks of you is written down before you lift it, with the reason attached, and you can hold him to it afterwards. If he is wrong about your body, you will be the first to know, because he tells you rather than quietly moving the target.

Where to start

Train for a block and check his work.

The fastest way to find out whether any of this is true for your body is to log real sets and read what he wrote down before each one. Read how it works for the loop itself, or take a receipt from your own phone to the verifier and check that nothing moved after the fact.
